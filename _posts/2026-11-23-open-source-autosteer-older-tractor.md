Imagine keeping the tractor you already know, while giving it the ability to follow a precise line across a field. That possibility is already being tried on working farms. For Caretaker, it opens a second route to longevity alongside repair. A machine can gain a capability that gives its owner another reason to keep using it.

In August 2026, Oliver Mark reported in *Farmers Weekly* on Tom Lamb’s AgOpenGPS installations, including a mid-1980s Massey Ferguson 698 sprayer tractor. Andy Ireland of RTK Precision helped with the work. The older tractor gained guidance and automatic turns through a hydraulic steering retrofit. Section control was still pending on that machine when the report was published. This is a reported working installation, with further upgrades still to come. [Read the reporting](https://www.fwi.co.uk/machinery/technology/how-to-get-ultra-accurate-rtk-guidance-for-one-off-1100-cost)

AgOpenGPS grew around Canadian farmer Brian Tischler and a community of contributors. Its public repositories provide the application, communications software, firmware and board designs. The application repository now labels its Windows Forms version as being in maintenance mode and directs new development towards Avalonia. Builders should record the exact software and firmware versions used in a working installation. [Application repository](https://github.com/AgOpenGPS-Official/AgOpenGPS) · [Hardware and firmware](https://github.com/AgOpenGPS-Official/Boards)

A tractor without a modern data bus can still be a candidate. The project documents steering-wheel motors and added hydraulic valves, alongside interfaces for machines already prepared for electronic steering. Position comes from a GNSS receiver and antenna. A controller connects that position and the guidance software to the steering hardware, with wheel-angle and orientation sensing appropriate to the installation. The choice depends on the actual steering system, mounting space and compatible components. [Steering options and hardware requirements](https://docs.agopengps.com/hardware/steering-options/)

Accurate position also needs a correction source. Centipede-RTK provides an open network of reference stations, supported by INRAE and other contributors. Check its live map and test reception at the intended field before budgeting around a shared station. Availability at a particular Irish farm needs local verification, including the connection that carries corrections to the tractor. [Centipede documentation and network map](https://docs.centipede-rtk.org/)

There is research behind the practical enthusiasm. Petri Linna and Hannu Haapala’s 2025 *Agronomy Research* case study describes a farmer-led AgOpenGPS and RTKBase implementation. Their hardware assessment put autosteer at €1,450–€2,350, and their first installation and configuration estimate totalled roughly 37 hours under favourable conditions. They explicitly describe the limited generalisability of a small case study. Those figures make useful planning evidence, with substantial room for differences between tractors and builders. [Read the open-access paper](https://doi.org/10.15159/AR.25.063)

A supplier provides another reference point. On 23 November 2026, Charles Quick’s CSEQ shop listed its hydraulic autosteer kit at £2,950 excluding VAT. It includes a controller, tablet, valve, wheel-angle sensor, orientation unit, antennas, wiring and engagement and emergency-stop controls. Machine-specific brackets and hydraulic hoses are extra. Confirm stock, compatibility, delivery, taxes, corrections and fitting in an actual quote. A kit price alone cannot establish the cost of a completed Irish installation. [Supplier’s kit description](https://shop.cseq.co.uk/products/complete-autosteer-kit-hydraulic)

Public discussion exposes some of the work behind the shopping list. In CSEQ’s January 2026 community thread, a contributor asks how to connect a valve to an existing controller board. That is a useful lead about integration friction. The manufacturer’s installation documentation and the tractor’s own hydraulic information must settle a particular build. [Community discussion](https://discourse.agopengps.com/t/hydraulic-valves-complete-kits-and-survey-sticks/20259)

The operator’s role matters. With display guidance, the person steers. With autosteer, hardware follows the line while the seated operator watches the machine and task. Remote operation moves the operator elsewhere. Full autonomy also needs a system capable of managing its defined task and responding to hazards. AgOpenGPS’s guidance calls for an attentive seated operator, tested disengagement and an immediate way to cut steering power. It also requires autosteer to be disabled for road travel. A field trial needs clear space, low-speed checks and a competent installer, with takeover tested again after configuration changes. [Project operating guidance](https://docs.agopengps.com/safety/)

Where could Caretaker help? Consider an Irish farmer keeping an older tractor for repeated spreading or spraying work. The CSO recorded 9,734 farms using GPS-guided machinery for plant protection in 2023. That establishes existing use of guidance, while demand for support on older machines remains a hypothesis. A commercial retrofit installer or a complete proprietary system is already an alternative. [CSO machinery survey](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-fss/farmstructuresurvey2023/farmmachineryandrenewableenergy/)

Try the smallest contribution on an existing AgOpenGPS installation. With the owner’s consent, record its components, versions, calibration, correction source and one recurring problem. Build an importer for a documented export into Caretaker’s proposed observation format. Preserve units, timestamps and missing data, and keep exact field locations private. Ask the installer whether that record makes the next support session shorter. Track both the time spent collecting it and any time saved. If it adds work without helping a repair or setup decision, revise the experiment. [Caretaker’s draft adapter contract](https://github.com/Hulupeep/caretaker/blob/main/docs/adapter-contract.md)

A possible paid service is ongoing support for a retrofit someone already owns. Its first test is one owner and installer finding the history useful enough to pay for another session. Caretaker’s proposed importer and that service still need building and testing.

**Have you fitted guidance to an older tractor? Bring the build, the awkward bracket, the calibration problem and what finally worked.** A documented machine profile or a small export sample with sensitive details removed would give the next owner a better starting point. [Share an experiment or propose a pull request](https://github.com/Hulupeep/caretaker)
