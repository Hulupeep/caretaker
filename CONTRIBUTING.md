# Contributing to Caretaker

**Help make it possible to keep the things we love working.** Caretaker starts with one T4, but the destination is much bigger: a community learning how to observe, maintain, repair and eventually restore many kinds of machines, with help from people, software, sensors and robots.

You do not need to be a programmer or own a T4. Mechanics, repairers, makers, engineers, researchers, designers, machine owners and curious beginners are all welcome.

## Ways to contribute

- **Bring a machine:** a bicycle, motorbike, boat, fridge, mixer, tractor, old computer or something else. Tell us what it is, what matters to you and what you already know.
- **Share an experiment:** sensor observations, inspection techniques, diagnostics, repair attempts and verified results—including the things that did not work.
- **Show us an existing project:** link to your own or someone else's relevant hardware, software, repair guide or research and explain what it actually does.
- **Build an adapter or tool:** help turn evidence into useful records, support a sensor or explore a specific machine interface.
- **Contribute repair knowledge:** correct an assumption, add sources, document a procedure or explain what requires a qualified specialist.
- **Improve the experience:** documentation, accessibility, graphics, product thinking and clearer ways for people to get involved.

## Start with an issue

Use the [machine or project form](https://github.com/Hulupeep/caretaker/issues/new?template=machine.yml) or the [experiment form](https://github.com/Hulupeep/caretaker/issues/new?template=experiment.yml). A short description, a question or a link is enough to start. You do not need to prepare a pull request first.

## Or open a pull request

1. Fork the repository and create a branch.
2. For a machine, copy [the machine profile template](machines/machine-template.md) to a clearly named path under `machines/`. For an experiment, copy [the experiment template](experiments/experiment-template.md) into `experiments/`.
3. Describe what you know, how you know it, what remains uncertain and what should happen next. Say whether evidence is real, simulated or proposed. Include unsuccessful or inconclusive results.
4. Submit a pull request describing the contribution and evidence. Link a related issue if there is one. A maintainer can discuss the contribution and suggest changes.

Small improvements and work in progress are welcome. A machine profile or project link records what is known; it is not a claim that Caretaker supports that machine.

## Attribution, privacy and responsible work

- Credit and link to sources. Include third-party code, manuals, images and other material only when its licence permits it; links and descriptions are useful alternatives. The Jeep scanner that inspired the project is GPL-3.0; check licence compatibility before reusing its code.
- Share only material you have permission to publish. Remove private identifiers, personal details, precise location, credentials and raw private records.
- Separate direct observations from interpretations. Include context, units, uncertainty and verification where possible. Never present simulated data as real.
- Begin with read-only observation and human-reviewed advice. Discuss changes involving actuation, machine controls or physical robotics before implementation.
- Road safety, mains electricity, batteries, pressure, fuel, refrigerants, lifting and other hazards require appropriate safeguards and qualified involvement. Do not treat an AI-generated procedure or a merged contribution as certification that a repair is safe.

## Where we're going

The [visual roadmap](docs/visual-roadmap.md) shows the first year and the long-term vision of a workshop that may one day carry out suitable repairs overnight. The vision belongs to everyone contributing; the milestones remain hypotheses until demonstrated.
