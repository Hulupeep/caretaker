# A 1970s tractor: give a mechanical machine senses

**Status: proposed reference profile.** No particular 1974 tractor has been inspected. Make, model, engine, electrical polarity, operating voltage, gauges and available connections must be established before specifying equipment. Many such machines may have little or no diagnostic electronics; assess the actual tractor.

## The opportunity

A farmer has a reliable old tractor but a breakdown costs time at precisely the wrong moment. Can a small set of observations help its owner and mechanic spot a change, preserve its history and work out what to check?

## Candidate observations

| What | Where to observe | Potential value | What it cannot tell us alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Battery voltage | Measure at battery terminals with a suitable meter; an installed logger would need vehicle-appropriate protection and qualified fitting. | Compare engine-off, starting and running behaviour under similar conditions. | Voltage alone does not prove battery health or locate a poor connection. |
| Engine surface temperature | Repeatable accessible external spot on the coolant outlet or engine casing, using a contact probe or thermal camera. | See how warm-up and operating temperature change. | Surface temperature is not necessarily coolant temperature; emissivity and placement matter. |
| Drips and hoses | Photo of the ground and visible hose/coupling areas during a parked inspection. | Track new marks or changes in leaks. | An image may not establish fluid type, source or severity. |
| Sound | Same microphone position and short recording at a comparable idle, with operating conditions recorded. | Compare a new rattle or squeal with earlier recordings. | A different sound does not identify a failed part. |
| Engine running time | Manually recorded hours or a suitable non-invasive runtime signal if verified for this machine. | Support service intervals. | Hours do not replace oil, belt or condition inspections. |
| Oil pressure | Existing gauge reading or specialist-fitted sensor at the manufacturer's approved point, if available. | Record readings at known temperature and speed. | A generic fitting, normal range or compatibility cannot be assumed. |

## First experiment

On a real tractor, ask the owner about a recurring concern. Record make and model, history, an engine-off battery reading or a photo, observation method and context in the [local log](../../tools/README.md). Repeat under comparable conditions; ask a mechanic whether the resulting history would help. No fault prediction is claimed.

## Installation notes

Do not disturb factory safety devices or fit a generic sensor to an unidentified connection. Old machines can differ in voltage and grounding. Make a model-specific plan and engage an appropriately qualified person for power or pressurised-system connections; secure equipment clear of heat, moving parts and operator controls.
