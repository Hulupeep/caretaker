# Plant machinery: a JCB-type backhoe or loader

**Status: sensor hypotheses, not a machine-specific installation guide.** JCB is an example manufacturer; no particular machine or partnership is implied. Identify make, model, serial range, manuals, electrical system and hydraulic circuit before design.

## The question

Could a machine owner or plant mechanic combine operating observations with service history to investigate an intermittent fault or change in hydraulic performance?

| Observation | Candidate location or method | What it may reveal | Limits / next check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hydraulic pressure | Manufacturer-designated test point, identified on the exact circuit and fitted by a hydraulic specialist with correctly rated equipment. | Compare pressure under a defined load and operating condition. | Pressure alone cannot locate a fault; relief settings and pressure limits are model-specific. Never loosen a pressurised line. |
| Hydraulic oil temperature | An approved measurement point or contact measurement on an accessible reservoir surface. | Compare warm-up or temperature changes during similar work. | Reservoir surface is not necessarily fluid temperature. |
| Cylinder drift | With the machine secured and isolated, follow the manufacturer's inspection method; measure position over time under a documented condition. | Establish whether a change warrants specialist investigation. | Loads and raised equipment are hazardous; only qualified people define and perform the test. |
| Leakage | Photographs of visible hoses, couplings and ground during a parked inspection. | Record where and when leaks appear. | A photo does not establish source or integrity; never search for leaks with a hand. |
| Electrical supply | Battery and charging system readings by a competent person, with engine state recorded. | Investigate supply variation during an intermittent fault. | Voltage alone cannot identify the cause. |
| Vibration / sound | Repeatable measurements at a suitably secured location, away from hot or moving parts, with operating mode documented. | Compare changes during the same task. | Signals are sensitive to location, load and speed; no generic failure threshold. |
| Thermal inspection | Operator or technician scans accessible housings after a comparable task. | Find unusual temperature patterns to investigate. | Reflections, sunlight and load can mislead; confirm using appropriate methods. |

## First experiment

Find an owner and independent plant mechanic. Pick one actual machine and a recent intermittent issue; review existing service notes and the manufacturer's diagnostic method. Create a private observation record and ask whether the brief saves time. Add sensors only where a specific question makes them worthwhile.

## Retrofit and autonomy

Keep **monitoring**, **operator assistance**, **remote operation** and **autonomous movement** as distinct projects. Do not assume a sensor gives safe control. Any later work on steering, hydraulics or motion needs a separate machine-specific engineering and verification plan.
