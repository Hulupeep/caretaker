# Product hypothesis

## Vision

A persistent caretaker helps a person keep an individual machine usable for as long as practical. It can connect observations from sensors, records, photographs, people and specialists; investigate; coordinate repair; and learn from outcomes. Future machines could include boats, tractors and generators.

## First user and machine

Colm, as owner or prospective buyer of a 1996 VW T4 2.5 TDI. Ownership, engine, ECU and diagnostic access are not yet confirmed. Start from whichever genuine vehicle information is available; clearly mark unknowns. Do not fabricate a machine history.

## Hypothesis

Given a genuine service history and at least one real observation, a persistent caretaker can help the owner understand what is known, what is unknown, and what to do next, with a brief a mechanic can use. Whether this prevents breakdowns or predicts failures is not established.

## Buyer hypothesis

T4 owners or independent specialists might pay for reliable ongoing care and a usable maintenance history. Test with a specific owner or specialist and a paid pilot ask. Open-source software and a future paid service can coexist; packaging and pricing are hypotheses to validate.

## Evidence and decisions

Every observation has a timestamp, source, units when applicable and context. Distinguish raw evidence, interpretation, possible explanations, unverified assumptions, and outcome. A missing sensor reading is not a healthy reading. Humans perform and verify physical checks. Do not infer a fault from a single image or value.

## Non-goals for first experiment

Universal breakdown prediction, ECU programming, autonomous driving, remote actuation, automated repair, continuous recording, and claims about detecting all faults.
