# Roadmap: a workshop that keeps things alive

## Destination

Caretaker becomes a persistent team of software agents, sensors, tools, people and, eventually, physical robots that look after the machines on your property. You leave an old bicycle, motorbike, boat component, 1950s Kenwood mixer or fridge in its care. Overnight, it inspects, researches, plans, repairs or restores what it is authorised and able to handle, tests the result and leaves a clear account for you in the morning. When it cannot safely or reliably proceed, it stops and asks for the right help.

Imagine never having to buy another car because the one you love can be understood, maintained, repaired and upgraded throughout your life. Now extend that idea to the other machines around you. Our ambition is to make “buy another one” a choice rather than the default—and to keep more useful things out of the scrapheap.

See the [visual timeline](visual-roadmap.md) for an evolving map of the journey. The stages below are invitations to build and experiment, with evidence marking the way. The T4 and a first physical repair can progress in parallel.

## 0. One machine, one care loop — now

- Establish Colm's actual T4 or a clearly identified prospective vehicle as the first vehicle case. Record identity, provenance, service history and unknowns.
- Add a real photo, inspection note, symptom or diagnostic export. Create an evidence-linked brief: observation, possible causes, next check, responsible person, and uncertainty.
- Record what the owner or mechanic actually finds and whether the recommendation helped.
- In parallel, select one genuinely available, low-risk household object with a simple issue as the first physical-repair candidate. Do not invent its condition.
- Gate: one real loop completed and independently checked; no simulated data presented as proof.

## 1. The digital caretaker

- Create a local-first machine passport, observations and repair timeline, attachment references, service calendar, parts list and owner preferences.
- Let a person ask questions, log symptoms and generate a practical inspection or mechanic brief.
- Every claim links to evidence and every unknown remains visible. Record wrong suggestions and actual outcomes.
- Gate: Colm can return later, understand what changed, and carry one case through to a verified result.

## 2. Eyes, ears and other senses

- Add one useful observation channel at a time: ordinary and thermal cameras, sound, vibration, voltage, pressure, fluid observations, or vehicle diagnostics where supported.
- Store sensor identity, time, units, placement, calibration and operating context; distinguish missing readings from normal readings.
- Compare like with like; validate detection and false alarms against known conditions and qualified inspection.
- Gate: demonstrate a specific observation is repeatable and useful on a real machine before claiming automated detection.

## 3. Restore and repair with people

- Build an investigation and repair plan from evidence, manuals, parts diagrams and expert input. Track uncertain part compatibility and source provenance.
- Produce checklists, required tools, estimated costs, approval requests, before-and-after images, tests and a repair report.
- A person does the work; Caretaker records deviations, tests and actual outcomes. Ask before purchases or contacting people.
- Gate: one completed, useful repair with a documented test, owner review and honest record of the limits.

## 4. The workshop develops hands

- Trial a fixed workbench with appropriate fixturing, cameras, guarded tools and a robot or existing automated device for one constrained low-risk operation.
- Start with known objects and repeatable tasks: for example, sorting verified screws or presenting a component for a human; later, an approved non-safety-critical fastener on a fixture.
- Human supervision, independent stop, clear workspace boundaries and physical safeguards are prerequisites. The system must detect uncertainty or a failed grasp and stop rather than improvise.
- Gate: repeated measured performance, documented failures and safe stop/recovery before expanding task or object variation.

## 5. Supervised restoration cells

- Combine inspection, fixture selection, tool changes and bounded actions in a workshop cell. A person approves the job plan and supervises initial runs.
- Learn from approved, verified procedures and repair outcomes; do not turn an unverified online guide into an instruction to act.
- Try one object family and one repair at a time: e.g., inspection and cleaning of a non-energised vintage mixer casing, or bicycle cleaning and non-safety-critical component handling.
- For roadworthiness, electrical safety, refrigerants, hazardous substances, lifting, fire risks and pressure systems, use appropriate competent specialists and independent verification. No unsupervised brake, structural, mains or refrigerant work by default.
- Gate: a complete, independently inspected repair record; no progression based only on successful-looking motion.

## 6. A workshop that can work overnight

- Schedule pre-approved, bounded jobs into a guarded space on the property. Confirm machine identity, condition, parts, safe isolation, tooling, emergency stop, monitoring and safe access before starting.
- Plan for power loss, network loss, sensor failure, dropped parts, unexpected resistance, fire/smoke and unsafe temperatures; stop, make the cell safe where possible and notify the owner.
- Begin with prevalidated low-risk tasks and no people or animals in the operating area. Remote work is never assumed safe simply because it is monitored.
- In the morning provide a job report: actions, evidence, tests, exceptions, remaining work, costs and any requirement for qualified inspection.
- Gate: extensive supervised operation and independent safety review for the specific cell and task before unattended trials. Scope increases only with evidence.

## 7. The lifetime caretaker

- Care for a household's or community's machines over years, including inventory, preventive inspection, repair, restoration, appropriate upgrades, verified parts sourcing and handover to specialists.
- Support categories incrementally: bicycles, motorbikes, boats, mixers, fridges, vehicles and others, each with distinct profiles, qualified work boundaries and verification methods.
- Evaluate repair, refurbishment, upgrade, reuse and responsible replacement using actual condition, lifetime cost and relevant environmental evidence. Repair is not assumed to be best in every case.
- Explore shared repair workshops, local specialists and open repair knowledge. Object history and private sensor data remain under owner control.
- Long-term aspiration: an affordable autonomous property workshop that can safely care for a growing range of machines overnight, while escalating unfamiliar or high-risk work.

## Commercial learning alongside every stage

Colm is the first user. Test whether other owners and independent repair specialists want the outcome using a genuine case and a paid-pilot ask. Record the buyer, problem, alternative, price discussion, objection and outcome. A personal experiment can continue without sales; substantial product investment requires evidence of demand.

## Current state

Documentation and an experiment plan. No T4-specific hardware, sensor detection, autonomous repair or overnight robot has been implemented or validated.
